FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

September 2025

What is the value of SEO?
SEO drives continuous results — a long-term investment that keeps delivering even after you've created content or optimized your site. Organic search results compound over time into clusters of hundreds of keywords per page. Content appreciates in value, especially when frequently updated and shared. SEO also ensures users have a positive experience through regular auditing and code updates.
How does SEO assist other marketing channels?
SEO and paid ads work together — paid search recovers lost organic clicks and provides keyword data for content optimization. SEO and social media keep your brand top-of-feed across platforms. SEO and email marketing work in tandem: organically ranked landing pages drive opt-ins that feed email lists, which send people back to the site, nurturing conversions.
How is SEO success measured at The Influence Agency?
  • Clicks & Impressions
  • Click-through rate
  • Average position
  • Total keywords in top 10
  • Total keywords per page
  • Total keywords per group
How do we report on SEO campaign efforts?
SEO campaign results are reported quarterly — looking back 90 days vs. the previous quarter and year. These are part of a larger quarterly report from your CSM every 3–4 months. At the monthly level, your CSM can provide dashboards for internal reporting needs. Form submissions and phone calls are tracked as goals/leads.
How do I calculate ROI with SEO?
ROI is calculated over a 12-month window. Example: $36,000/year retainer, 15 organic leads/month, 10 convert at $500/month each.

Total Leads = 180 · SEO Investment = $36,000 · SEO Return = $60,000 · Gain = $24,000 · ROI = 66% · Cost Per Lead = $200
Reference

Glossary

Key SEO terms defined

AMP
Accelerated Mobile Pages — a coding method that strips back visual elements to load pages faster, with some evidence of a small Google SEO boost.
Anchor Text
The actual written text that is hyperlinked. Carries weight in Google's eyes and should be optimised for SEO benefits.
Backlink
A link pointing "back" to a target website from another domain, passing page authority and giving Google context about the domain.
Bounce Rate
Percentage of users who land on your site and immediately leave without interacting. High bounce rate indicates poor content or design.
Citation
A specific backlink that reiterates Name, Address, and Phone Number (NAP). Important for providing Google with trust signals for local SEO.
CMS
Content Management System — the user-friendly platform sitting on top of website code, allowing uploading and editing of content.
Domain Rating (DR)
An SEO ranking score that predicts how well a website will rank on SERPs, ranging from 1 to 100.
HTML Sitemap
An easily readable page listing the structure of a website for easier human navigation.
Keyword
A phrase used during an internet search. A business wants to show up at the top of Google for relevant keywords.
NAP-W
Name, Address, Phone Number, Website — a key trust signal for Google requiring absolute consistency across the internet. Mismatches result in SEO penalties.
Nofollow
A backlink type that instructs Google not to follow the link. Does not pass authority, though some nofollow links benefit a natural backlink profile.
Robot.txt
A custom text file attached to a domain instructing Google's robots how to crawl the website and which pages not to index.
Schema Markup
Code added to a website giving Google further context about its content. Allows for rich search results like review stars and featured snippets.
SSL
Secure Sockets Layer — standard security technology for an encrypted browser-server link. Google penalizes non-secure URLs.
XML Sitemap
A file uploaded to a website giving Google's robots a map of the site structure — the machine-readable equivalent of an HTML sitemap.
